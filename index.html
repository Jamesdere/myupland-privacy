My Upland — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 1 October 2026

My Upland is a hunting journal. This explains what it keeps, what leaves your phone, and how to get rid of all of it.

It is written to be read rather than to be survived. If anything here is unclear, that is a fault worth reporting.

The short version

  • Your journal is yours. It is not sold, not shared, and not used to advertise anything.
  • Nothing tracks you. There is no advertising, no analytics, and no third-party tracking of any kind in this app.
  • The private parts of a record are encrypted on your phone before they are sent anywhere, with a key only you hold.
  • Your photographs stay in your own photo library. The app never uploads them.
  • You can delete your account and everything in it from inside the app, at any time.

What you give us

An email address and a password, to make an account. The address is how you sign in and how you reset a forgotten password. It is not used to send marketing.

An account is required before any record can be saved. That is so a season's journal can survive a lost phone — it is kept on a server so it can be given back to you.

What the app keeps about your hunting

A record of a hunt or a scouting trip, which may include the date and time, the bird you were after, what you saw and what came down, the cover, the dogs and the people with you, the weather at the time, where it happened, and anything you wrote down.

What is encrypted before it leaves your phone

These are scrambled on the device with a key that only you hold. We cannot read them:

  • Exactly where you were — the precise coordinates of a hunt, a flush or a place.
  • Everything you wrote — notes on the day, on the dogs, on your gear, and the headline of a hunt.
  • The names of the people you hunt with, and what you call them. They are somebody else's name, and they never agreed to be in this app.
  • Bird counts.

What is not encrypted

So the app can sort, group and read your season back to you, some things are stored plainly on the server:

  • An approximate area — a short code that narrows a position to roughly a neighbourhood, not a stand.
  • The name you gave a place, as you typed it.
  • Dates and times, the species, the cover type, how far you walked, and how many birds were recovered.

That distinction is the honest one and it is worth reading twice. Your exact coordinates are unreadable to us; the name you gave a place and the rough area are not.

Your photographs

The app never uploads a photograph. It stores a reference to a picture in your own iOS photo library and nothing more. If you delete the photo from your library it is gone — the app only ever held a pointer to it.

Who else sees anything

A weather service. To show what the conditions were, the app asks Open-Meteo about a position. It receives a coordinate and a date, and nothing about you — no account, no name, no identifier.

Our own server. Your records are stored on a database we run, hosted by Supabase, so they can be restored to a new phone.

Nobody else. There is no advertising network, no analytics service, and no crash reporter. We do not sell, rent or share your journal, and there is no business arrangement under which anybody could ask us for it.

Your location

The app asks for your location so it can record where a hunt happened and fetch the weather there. It is only ever read while you are using the app — never in the background and never when it is closed.

You can refuse. The app still works; records are logged without a position.

Deleting it

In the app: Profile → Delete Account. It removes your account and every record attached to it from our server. It tells you what it is about to delete before it does it.

This cannot be undone, which is the point of it.

Your encryption key

The key that scrambles the private parts of your journal is created on your phone. A copy is kept on the server, locked by your password, so that signing in on a new phone can give it back to you.

A recovery key is offered when you create an account. If you forget your password and have no recovery key, the encrypted parts of your journal cannot be recovered by us or by anyone else. That is what it means for the key to be yours.

Children

My Upland is not intended for children and does not knowingly collect anything from them.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it.
